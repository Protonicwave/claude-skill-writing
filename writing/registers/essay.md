# Essay register

Essays, reflective pieces and argued answers to a set question.

## The target

An argument a reader can follow and disagree with. This register has the most room for narrative and for the writer's own voice, but every claim still needs its evidence.

## Rules

- **Answer the question early.** The position goes in the first paragraph, in one or two sentences a reader could quote back. Restate the set question only if its terms need defining.
- **One claim per paragraph.** The first sentence says what the paragraph argues; the rest gives the evidence and reasoning; the last sentence lands the point, not a summary of it.
- **Evidence over assertion.** Specific cases, numbers, dates and named work, each verified under `sources.md`. A generic example ("in many fields") is a sign the paragraph has no evidence yet.
- **Name the strongest objection and answer it.** State it fairly, in the form its best advocate would use, then say why the position survives or where it has to give ground.
- **History as story.** When background is needed, tell it with names and dates: who tried what, what failed, what changed.
- **First person where it carries weight.** "I think" for a judgement the essay defends; not as a softener.
- **The conclusion answers the question.** It says what the argument has established and what it leaves open. It does not list the paragraphs again.

## Reflective writing

Reflective pieces (on a project, a placement, a group's work) follow the same rules with one addition: describe what happened specifically before evaluating it. "The first design used one queue per ship and stalled at 40 ships" is reflection; "I learnt a lot about design" is not.
