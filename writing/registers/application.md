# Application register

Cover letters, personal statements and supporting statements for internships, jobs, placements, master's and PhD places, and scholarships.

## The target

A selector with a list of criteria and a pile of applications. They read for evidence that the applicant meets each criterion, and they have seen every stock phrase. The letter that works reads like the student explaining, specifically and without fuss, why their record fits this role.

## Before drafting

- **Read the advert first.** List its essential and desirable criteria, the project or group named, the person to address, and any limit on length or format. A person specification with numbered criteria sets the structure: a supporting statement answers them in their order, under their headings if the advert asks for that.
- **Map each criterion to the student's record.** For each one, the experience from the curriculum vitae (CV) or notes that shows it: what the student did, with what, and the result, with a number where the notes have one. Two or three strong matches beat every criterion touched once.
- **A criterion with no evidence stays without it.** Never claim a skill, result, grade, tool or experience the notes do not contain, and never stretch one ("used Python" does not become "developed a Python package"). Leave it out, or name it honestly as something the student would learn in the role, and list it in the delivery note so the student can supply evidence if they have it.

## Rules

- **Open on the role and the specific connection.** The first sentence names the position and the one thing that links the student to it: the project, the group's method, the experience that matches. Not "I am thrilled to apply", not a sentence about the field's importance.
- **Evidence, not self-description.** "In my final-year project I measured the coherence time of ..." shows a skill; "I am a hard-working, passionate physicist" shows nothing. Every claim about the student is something they did, and adjectives about themselves go.
- **One paragraph per strong match.** Its first sentence names the criterion's substance, the rest gives the experience and its result, and the last sentence says what it means for this role.
- **Show knowledge of the role from the advert, not from memory.** Name the project, group or method as the advert does. Do not describe a group's research, people or publications from memory: anything beyond the advert needs a source the student supplies or one verified under `sources.md`.
- **Confident and plain.** First person throughout. State achievements without apology and without inflation (`voice.md`, confidence). No "I believe I would be a great fit", "unique opportunity", "esteemed", "dynamic team", "I am confident that".
- **Close on what happens next.** What the student would bring to the specific work, availability or start date if the notes give one, and a plain sign-off. One sentence of thanks at most.

## Form

- **Address.** The named person from the advert, as "Dear Dr Patel" or "Dear Professor Byrne". "Yours sincerely" after a named person, "Yours faithfully" after "Dear Sir or Madam". Use a name only if the advert or the student supplies it.
- **Length.** A cover letter fits one page: about 250 to 400 words in four or five paragraphs. A personal or supporting statement follows its stated limit in words or characters; count, and cut adjectives and repeated context before evidence.
- **Format.** Prose paragraphs. No bullet lists in a cover letter. Headings only in a supporting statement that the advert asks to follow its criteria.
- **One letter per advert.** Nothing in the letter should fit another advert unchanged.

## Personal statements for research degrees

A PhD or master's statement adds three things. The research question the student wants to work on, specific enough that a supervisor can judge fit. The student's own research so far: what they did, what they found, what went wrong and what they changed, told with the numbers. And why this group or programme, from what the advert or the student's notes say about it.

## Delivery note

After the letter: the criteria it does not evidence, any claim the student should check against their record, and anything the advert asks for that the letter leaves to them (a portfolio, referees, a reference number).
