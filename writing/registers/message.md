# Message register

Emails, Teams and Slack posts, and messages to supervisors, teammates or clients.

## The target

A message a busy person reads once and acts on. It sounds like the student on a good day: direct, polite, specific, and short.

## Rules

- **The first line carries the point**: the request, the decision needed or the news. Context comes after, and only as much as the reader needs to act.
- **Several decisions, numbered.** "Could the meeting confirm three things: (1) the energy model, (2) point-mass fleets for movement, (3) any changes to the design?" The reader can answer "yes to 1 and 3".
- **Say what happens next and when.** "I'll hold the architecture update until then."
- **Match the relationship.** A supervisor gets "Dear Dr Surname" or "Hi Firstname" as the student already uses; a teammate gets no greeting in a chat thread. Follow the tone of the thread being replied to.
- **No openers or closers that say nothing.** No "I hope this email finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "Just circling back", "Certainly". A plain "Thanks" or "Best wishes" closes an email. "Let me know if that doesn't work" is fine when it is a real invitation.
- **No formatting in a chat message.** No bold, no headings, no bullet lists for three sentences of content. A short email may use a numbered list for decisions.
- **Specific over polite filler.** "The draft is attached; section 3 is the one I'm least sure about" beats "Please find attached the draft for your review and feedback".
- **Rephrasing the student's draft.** Keep their meaning and their level of formality; change only what reads as stiff, vague or machine-made. Keep it about the same length or shorter.

## Example

Stiff:

> I hope you are well. I wanted to reach out to provide an update regarding the progress of the architecture work. Furthermore, it would be greatly appreciated if the team could confirm several key decisions at the upcoming meeting.

Natural:

> Could the meeting confirm three things: (1) the energy model, or an alternative, (2) point-mass fleets for movement, and (3) any changes to the design? I'm holding the architecture update until then.
