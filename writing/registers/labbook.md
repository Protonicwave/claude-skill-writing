# Lab book register

Lab book entries, research diaries and notes kept as a record of the work.

## The target

A record a supervisor or examiner can audit: what was done, when, why, what came out and what happens next. It is evidence of process, so it must be accurate to what happened, including the false starts. Polish is not the point; legibility and honesty are.

## Rules

- **Dates come from the record.** Use the dates in the student's notes, commit history, file timestamps or meeting records. Never invent or round a date. If one is missing, write `[DATA NEEDED: date of ...]`.
- **One entry per session or decision.** Each entry has the date, a one-line heading, then four parts in prose or short labelled lines: what was done, why it was done, what it produced (numbers, file names, figure references), and the next step.
- **Write as it happened.** Past tense for actions. Record mistakes and dead ends with the reason they failed; a lab book that only records successes is not believable.
- **Justify decisions at the time they were made.** "Chose a 10 mW step because the earlier 50 mW step skipped the threshold" is the kind of sentence an examiner looks for.
- **Point to the evidence.** Name the script, the data file, the commit or the printed figure and where it is stuck in. "Figure 4 (printed, stuck opposite this page)".
- **Keep it short.** Notes, not essays. Full sentences, but no introductions and no conclusions.
- **Separate the record from later reflection.** If something is understood later, add a dated note rather than rewriting the old entry.

## Example of the shape

> 14 October 2026. Threshold scan, first pass.
> Swept pump power from 0.1 W to 2.0 W in 0.1 W steps to find where the two-lobed pattern first appears. The pattern appeared between 1.5 W and 1.7 W, too coarse to fix the threshold. Raw images in `data/2026-10-14/`; summary plot is Figure 3. Next: repeat between 1.4 W and 1.8 W in 10 mW steps.

The numbers in a real entry come from the student's data, never from this example.
