# Explainer register

Lay summaries, general-audience summaries, outreach pieces and anything written for a reader outside physics or computing.

## The target

An educated reader who is not a specialist finishes knowing what was done, why it was hard, what was found and why it matters, and has learnt the field's real words on the way. Durham's guidance for a 200-word general audience summary puts the trap plainly: it is not "restating your work with the technical terms removed". It is a narrative account of what the work does and why it is important.

## Rules

- **Tell it as a story of a question.** The question in ordinary terms; why it is hard; what was done; what was found; what it means and what is still unknown. That order works from 200 words to 2,000.
- **Keep the precise term, glossed.** "An Ising machine, a device that finds the lowest-energy arrangement of many interacting magnets, ..." Then use exactly that term every time (`clarity.md`).
- **Concrete first.** Start from something the reader can picture or count, then generalise.
- **Make numbers tangible by comparison, not by adjective.** "More possible arrangements than there are atoms in the observable universe", when that is true and checkable.
- **At most one analogy**, under the rule in `clarity.md`: exact, shorter than the literal explanation, and with its breaking point stated.
- **No equations** unless the piece is for a reader who asked for them. Say in words what the equation says.
- **Honest scale.** Say how big the result is and how sure, in words the reader can weigh: "in simulations of up to 64 magnets", not "a breakthrough".
- **Ending.** The open question, or what the work makes possible next. Not a summary.

## Length

Lay summaries usually have a hard limit (often 200 words). Count, and cut adjectives before content.
