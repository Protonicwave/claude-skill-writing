# Post register

Blog posts, LinkedIn posts and newsletter items: writing published to a mixed audience that chooses, line by line, whether to keep reading.

## The target

A reader scrolling past who stops because the first line says something specific, and leaves knowing what was done and one thing that was found. The voice is the student talking to colleagues and friends at once: plain, specific and a little personal, never a press release.

## Rules

- **Open on the thing itself.** What was made, found, submitted or learnt, with one concrete detail, in the first sentence. The first two lines are all most readers see before the post folds, so they carry the news. Not "I'm thrilled to announce", "humbled", "excited to share", a one-word hook line, or a question asked only to hook.
- **One concrete result.** A number, an image of the apparatus, a before-and-after: something from the student's notes that a reader could repeat to someone else. Explain any technical term in passing, as the explainer register does (`registers/explainer.md`).
- **Paragraphs, not a stack of lines.** Most paragraphs hold two to four sentences. A run of one-sentence paragraphs is the template of the generated post; use a one-line paragraph only where the content is one line.
- **Thank people by name and for what they did.** "Thanks to Dr Mensah for weekly meetings that caught two wrong assumptions early." Once, without superlatives, and only people the notes name. Do not name classmates or collaborators in a public post unless the notes say they agreed.
- **Feelings after facts, briefly.** A sentence on what the work meant to the student is fine once the reader knows what the work was. Gratitude and pride shown by specifics, not stated three ways.
- **No engagement bait.** No "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Drop a comment", "Like if". A real question the student wants answered is fine, once, at the end.
- **End on what comes next**: the next project, the open problem, where to read the full work. Not a summary, not a motto.

## Form

- **LinkedIn.** About 80 to 200 words. At most three hashtags, at the end, ones people actually follow. Links in the post only if the student wants them there.
- **Blog post.** A title that states the finding or the question, not a pun. Headings only beyond about 800 words. Link every source; facts and figures follow `sources.md` as in any other register.
- **Newsletter item.** The thing a reader may need to act on goes first, with its date. One item per paragraph, each with a plain heading if there are several.
- No emoji, no bold for emphasis, no arrows or symbols as bullets.

## Facts in public

A post is public and permanent. Every number comes from the student's notes. The student's own results from their own project are theirs to share, so the headline one goes in. What belongs to someone else stays out unless the notes say it may be shared: a research group's or company's unpublished results, anything under embargo or a confidentiality agreement, other people's marks or details. Never claim impact the work has not shown ("could revolutionise"). When a result might be restricted, for example because the project used a collaboration's private data, keep it in and say in the delivery note what the student should check before posting.
