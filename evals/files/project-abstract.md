Monte Carlo modelling of light transport in skin for wearable pulse oximetry

Pulse oximeters estimate arterial oxygen saturation (SpO2) from the ratio of red (660 nm) to infrared (940 nm) light absorbed by pulsing blood. Their readings are biased high in people with darker skin because melanin in the epidermis absorbs red light more strongly than infrared. We wrote a GPU Monte Carlo code that tracks 10^8 photons through a five-layer skin model and computed the ratio-of-ratios calibration curve for epidermal melanin volume fractions from 1% to 43%. At a true saturation of 85%, the standard calibration overestimated saturation by 0.4 percentage points for the lightest skin and 3.1 percentage points for the darkest. Moving the detector 2 mm further from the source reduced the largest bias to 1.2 points, at the cost of 38% less detected signal.
